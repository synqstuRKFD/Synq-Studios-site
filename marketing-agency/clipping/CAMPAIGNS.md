## 1. Content Rewards (formerly Whop Content Rewards)

**What it is.** A marketplace where brands and creators fund clipping and UGC campaigns and pay creators for approved views. It started on Whop in late 2024. Content Rewards Inc. was formed in June 2025, and it launched its own app at contentrewards.com in August 2026. Whop still processes the payouts. Sources: [About page, updated 2026-11-18](https://contentrewards.com/about), [Whop docs](https://docs.whop.com/memberships-and-access/third-party-apps/content-rewards.md).

**How payment works**
- CPM earnings = (counted views / 1,000) x rate, up to a per-clip maximum. A clip earns nothing until it passes the campaign's minimum payout. Views are counted on your post, not on the source video. Earnings settle in rounds: 7 days of earning, then a 3-day hold (about every 10 days). A clip earns for 30 days after you submit it. [Payouts explained, 2026-11-09](https://contentrewards.com/articles/clipping-payouts-explained), [FAQ](https://contentrewards.com/faqs)
- Rates: median $1.00/1K across 710 live campaigns. Half of campaigns list between $1.00 and $2.00. By platform: TikTok $1.00, IG $1.00, YouTube $1.10, X $1.00. The median minimum payout floor is $2.00. These are listed rates before the 10% fee. Snapshot dated 2026-11-16. [How much do clippers make](https://contentrewards.com/articles/how-much-do-clippers-make)
- Brand guidance says most filled campaigns pay $0.80 to $2.00/1K, with about a $200 per-clip cap being typical. [Payouts explained](https://contentrewards.com/articles/clipping-payouts-explained)
- Fees: 10% creator fee on CPM and pool campaigns. Per-post and retainer campaigns with a budget of $5,000 or more charge no fee. Legacy campaigns may still show a 7% fee. [Creator Terms, eff. 2026-11-17](https://www.contentrewards.com/terms-of-service/clippers), [Payouts explained](https://contentrewards.com/articles/clipping-payouts-explained)
- Withdrawal: moving money from the Content Rewards balance to your Whop balance is free from $1. From Whop you can withdraw to a bank, PayPal or wallet. Standard withdrawals take up to 5 business days; instant withdrawals carry a fee. [Payouts explained](https://contentrewards.com/articles/clipping-payouts-explained)
- Budget risk: payouts stop when a campaign pauses or runs out of budget, or when a clip hits its cap. [Payouts explained](https://contentrewards.com/articles/clipping-payouts-explained)
- Tax forms (W-9/1099): not published in the Creator Terms.

**Requirements**
- Age 18+. Minors are not allowed, even with parental consent. One account per creator. [Creator Terms §2.1, §13.3](https://www.contentrewards.com/terms-of-service/clippers)
- Account age and follower minimums: not published. Some campaigns are open to everyone; others require an application. [FAQ](https://contentrewards.com/faqs)
- Submit your post link within 30 minutes of posting. Nothing is approved automatically; the brand reviews every submission. [FAQ](https://contentrewards.com/faqs)
- View verification: every submission gets a 0 to 100 bot score. A high score pauses the payout, but a person makes the final decision. Views come from public counts that are read on a schedule. [About](https://contentrewards.com/about), [Payouts explained](https://contentrewards.com/articles/clipping-payouts-explained)

**Permissions and disclosure**
- You are solely responsible for FTC compliance. Disclosure must be "clear and conspicuous" and, in videos, at the beginning. Content Rewards does not check clips for FTC compliance, and a non-compliant clip can be rejected and lose its pay. Political clips need a disclosure and cannot go on TikTok or X. [Creator Terms §12.9, §12.11](https://www.contentrewards.com/terms-of-service/clippers)
- You must clear third-party content, including music. For clips of brand-provided footage, you assign rights in the final clip to the brand once it is approved. [Creator Terms §17.2, §17.6](https://www.contentrewards.com/terms-of-service/clippers)
- Listed rejection reasons: wrong platform, missing tags or disclosure, reused content, editing or deleting the post after you submit it. [FAQ](https://contentrewards.com/faqs)

**Botting rules and red flags**
- Buying or generating fake views is banned. The penalty is a permanent ban (appealable once), blocking of your linked social accounts, reversal of unsettled payouts, and offsets for confirmed fraud. [Creator Terms §12, §13, §11](https://www.contentrewards.com/terms-of-service/clippers)
- Complaints, reported by a competitor (FindClout, 2026-08-26): a Trustpilot review dated 2026-07-26 says a $196.63 balance stayed "Upcoming" for more than a month. A forum post says approvals are slow and calls Whop full of "scammers." Clippers also report being flagged for botting near payout thresholds; FindClout itself says it could not verify these reports. [FindClout complaints roundup](https://findclout.com/blog/content-rewards-complaints)
- A campaign can still reject a clip after the views have come in. Rejected clips earn nothing. [Payouts explained](https://contentrewards.com/articles/clipping-payouts-explained)

## 2. Vyro (MrBeast)

- **What it is:** MrBeast's platform that pays for clips meeting creator or brand briefs. MrBeast and Mark Rober were named as early creators. [Tubefilter, 2025-10-14](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/10/14/mrbeast-vyro-clipping-platform-viewstats-expansion/)
- **Pay:** $3 per 1,000 views in the King of Meat launch campaign. Payouts were described as hourly, withdrawable to crypto wallets, PayPal or banks. [Tubefilter, 2025-10-14](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/10/14/mrbeast-vyro-clipping-platform-viewstats-expansion/). The official site says brands set a rate per 1K *verified* views and a budget cap; it gives no current clipper rate, minimum or payout method. [vyro.com](https://vyro.com)
- **Requirements:** You connect your social accounts so Vyro can read metrics directly, post from your own accounts and follow the brief. Follower and account-age minimums are not published. Platforms: TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, X. [vyro.com](https://vyro.com)
- **Permissions and disclosure:** Each brand's brief sets its own disclosure rules. Off-brief posts are removed. [vyro.com](https://vyro.com)
- **Fraud:** Posts pass compliance and fraud checks before their views count, and flagged views are not paid. Penalties are not published. [vyro.com](https://vyro.com)
- **Red flags or complaints:** I found none from primary sources. A competitor blog claims listed campaigns paid $1 to $2/1K rather than $3; I could not verify this, so it is not in the table.

## 3. Clipster (clipster.gg now redirects to clipster.com)

- **What it is:** A CPM clipping marketplace. Brands set the CPM and budget, and Clipster charges brands a 20% fee. It claims $10M+ paid to creators and 460K+ creators (self-reported). [clipster.com](https://www.clipster.com/)
- **Pay:** Clipper rates are not published on the official site. A campaign ends when its budget runs out, then a 24 to 48 hour final review runs before earnings reach your wallet. Each video must reach the campaign's minimum views on its own; views do not combine across videos. Withdrawals go to PayPal, bank, crypto or gift cards, with a 5% withdrawal fee. [clipster.com](https://www.clipster.com/)
- **Requirements:** You connect accounts through official OAuth login and submit each link. "No follower minimums." The iOS app (Saikyo, LLC) is rated 13+ in the App Store, but the clipper minimum age is not published. [clipster.com](https://www.clipster.com/), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6746810841)
- **Disclosure:** Not published. Music campaigns require the exact sound provided, at minimum volume levels. [clipster.com](https://www.clipster.com/)
- **Fraud:** Posts are checked at submission and while live, for bought views, unnatural engagement ratios and suspicious audiences. Offending accounts are suspended, and appeals go through Discord. [clipster.com](https://www.clipster.com/)
- **Complaints:** App Store rating 4.5/5 (120 ratings). The one payment-related review (a wrong PayPal email) was resolved. [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6746810841)

## 4. Creator- or platform-run programs

- **Kick Clipping Program:** Clippers post streamers' moments to social media. Any Kick streamer can join through Kick's Discord application portal. Kick discourages "clip farming" and says the program drew 3B+ clip views in September. Pay rates and payout structure: not published. [win.gg, 2025-12-11](https://win.gg/kick-clipping-program/)
- Many individual creators run their campaigns *through* Content Rewards or Vyro rather than separately (for example, MrBeast on Vyro). Before working for a standalone creator Discord, confirm the program on the creator's own verified channels.

## General scam patterns to avoid

- **Pay-to-join is the clearest red flag.** That includes joining fees, paid Discord tiers, "verification deposits," courses you must buy first, and crypto "gas fees." "Real clipping never charges the clipper to participate." [FindClout, 2026](https://findclout.com/blog/is-clipping-a-scam), [FindClout red flags, 2026-07-22](https://findclout.com/blog/clipper-red-flags) (a competitor's blog, but this advice matches Discord's policy below)
- **Fake campaign DMs** that ask for your password, 2FA codes or "manager access," or that keep the terms only inside the DM. [FindClout Discord scams](https://findclout.com/blog/clipping-discord-scams)
- **No written payout terms, view counts you can't verify (screenshots only), approval rules that change after you submit, guaranteed earnings, pressure to hurry.** [FindClout red flags](https://findclout.com/blog/clipper-red-flags)
- **Advance-fee fraud** is listed as a prohibited financial scam under Discord's policy. [Discord deceptive practices policy, 2024-03-15](https://discord.com/safety/deceptive-practices-policy-explainer). Discord also warns, "If it sounds too good to be true, it probably is." [Discord blog, 2022-02-03](https://discord.com/blog/common-scams-what-to-look-out-for)
- **View-botting:** it gets you banned on the platforms (permanent ban and linked-account blocks on Content Rewards; suspension on Clipster). The FTC's 2024 rule also prohibits buying fake indicators of social media influence, "such as followers or views," for commercial purposes. [FTC, 2024-08-14](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)

## Disclosure basics (US)

Paid clips are endorsements. Use plain words like "ad" or "sponsored" (#ad and #sponsored are fine), and avoid "sp," "spon" and "collab." In videos, put the disclosure in the video itself, not only in the description, ideally in both audio and on screen. [FTC Disclosures 101, Nov 2019](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers). Content Rewards also requires the disclosure at the start of the clip. [Creator Terms §12.9](https://www.contentrewards.com/terms-of-service/clippers)
